# Journals and pages

There are two kinds of place to write, and they have different jobs.

## The journal: where you write

Each day has a page of its own, named by its date. tlog opens on today's.

The journal is where almost everything should go. You do not decide where a
note belongs before writing it; you write it *now*, on today's page, and say
what it is about by [linking](links.md) and [tagging](tags.md).

This removes the question that kills most note-taking systems — "where do I put
this?" The answer is always: here, today.

A day's file exists only once you have written something on that day. Stepping
through empty days creates nothing.

## Pages: where things collect

A page has a name instead of a date: a person, a project, a topic, a book.

A page has two parts:

1. **Its own outline** — things you wrote directly on it. Good for what is
   timeless about the subject: a phone number, a project's goal, a reading
   list.
2. **Everything that mentions it** — every block elsewhere that links to this
   page, listed below with its children. This part writes itself.

For many pages, the first part stays empty forever, and that is fine. Ada's
page can be nothing but the forty times you mentioned her, in order.

## How a page comes into being

You do not "create" pages. One appears when you

- link to it — `@Ada Lovelace` or `[[Ada Lovelace]]` — or
- go to it — `p`, type a new name, `enter`.

Until you write on the page itself, there is not even a file for it. It exists
because something points at it.

## Which to use

| You are… | Write it |
|---|---|
| noting something that happened, was said, or occurred to you | in the journal, linking what it concerns |
| recording a lasting fact about a subject | on its page |
| not sure | in the journal |

When in doubt, the journal is right, because a journal note that links to a
page shows up on that page anyway. The reverse is not true: something written
only on a page carries no date and no context.

## More than one name

A page can answer to several names. On Ada's page, `/alias Ada` makes `[[Ada]]`
and `@ada` mean her; mentions under either name land on the same page.

## A day is a page too

`@fri` writes a link to Friday's journal. On Friday, that block is listed under
the day's own notes. Linking to a day is the simplest reminder there is.
